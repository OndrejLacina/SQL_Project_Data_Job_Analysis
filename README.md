# 📊 Data Analyst Job Market Analysis 2023-2025: SQL Portfolio Project

## 🎯 Project Overview

This project explores the data analyst job market from 2023 to 2025, analyzing top-paying roles & companies (remotely), in-demand skills, and the intersection of salary and market demand. The ultimate goal is to move beyond simple job counts and engineer a custom ROI (Return on Investment) model to identify the most optimal technologies an aspiring Data Analyst should master.

## 🛠️ Tools & Technologies

- **SQL (PostgreSQL):** Engineered complex queries utilizing Data Pipelining (multiple CTEs), `CROSS JOIN` for market-share calculations, multiple `INNER`/`LEFT` `JOIN`s, `CASE WHEN` statements for business logic categorization, aggregations (`COUNT`, `AVG`, `ROUND`, dividing), filtering (`WHERE`, `HAVING`), data formatting, logical sorting.
- **Database Architecture:** Utilizing a star schema consisting of a main fact table (`job_postings_fact`) and supporting dimension tables (`skills_dim`, `company_dim`).
- **VS Code:** My primary IDE for executing SQL scripts and database management.
- **Git & GitHub:** Version control, repository management, and project sharing.
- **Gemini Pro:** my personal lecturer and made with it visualizations

## ✅ The 5 Core Questions Answered

1. **Top-Paying Companies:** What are the top 25 highest-paying Data Analyst companies available remotely?
2. **Elite Skill Requirements:** What specific technical skills do these top-paying companies demand (remote-jobs)?
3. **Skill Demand Globally:** What are the most frequently requested skills for Data Analysts across the entire job market?
4. **Avg Salary Per Skill:** What is the average pay for each skill?
5. **The Optimal ROI Skill:** Which skills offer the most optimal return on investment (ROI) in terms of market demand and salary?

#### **5. The Optimal ROI Skill Matrix for Data Analysts**

[SQL Query5 - LINK](/project_sql/5_most_optimal_ROI_skills.sql)

To answer this, I engineered a custom market-share model. Rather than looking at raw posting counts, I calculated the **demand rate** (percentage of total job postings requiring the skill) and categorized them into five distinct tiers. This framework helps identify true market value:

- **1) Essentials:** High Demand (>18%) & High Pay (>$100k)
- **2) Fundamental (or Demand):** High Demand (>18%) & Lower Pay (<$100k)
- **3) Premium Skill:** Lower Demand (<18%) & High Pay (>$100k)
- **4) Solid Choice:** Lower Demand (<18%) & Solid Pay (>$90k)
- **5) Low Value:** Low Demand & Low Pay (<$90k)

## 💡 Key Business Insights

- **The Core Foundation:** The absolute baseline is **SQL**, appearing in the vast majority of job postings, paired closely with **Data Visualization Tools** (Tableau / Power BI) and **Python** for advanced analysis. However, **Excel** maintains a massive market presence and remains an essential prerequisite, especially for entry-to-mid-level Data Analyst roles.
- **Beware of the "Niche Trap":** While highly specialized skills (like obscure cloud tools or specific coding languages) show massive average salaries, their actual market demand is often extremely low. Relying purely on salary data without weighting it against posting volume (`COUNT` and Demand Rate) creates a dangerously skewed perspective.
- **Regional & Ecosystem Strategy:** While the global remote market heavily features tools like Looker or Snowflake, enterprise and European markets are deeply rooted in the Microsoft ecosystem. A foundational stack of **SQL, Excel, and Power BI** is the safest and most strategic entry point before branching into premium cloud technologies.
